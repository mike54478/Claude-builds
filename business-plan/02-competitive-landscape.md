# Competitive Landscape

Competition here validates the market. The question is not "is there competition" — it's "is there a
defensible seam for a fast solo founder." There is: **deep vertical (plumbing/HVAC), SMB price
point, done-for-you setup, and booking (not message-taking) as the core promise.**

## The four competitor classes

### 1. Human answering services (the real incumbent we displace)
**Ruby, Smith.ai, AnswerConnect, PATLive, local answering bureaus**
- Price: $250–$700+/mo, metered by minutes or calls. Overages sting.
- What they do: answer, take a message, maybe basic intake. They do NOT triage emergencies well,
  do NOT book into FSM software, and quality varies by whoever picks up.
- Weakness we exploit: we cost less, answer instantly at 2am with zero hold time, and *book the
  job*. The sales pitch writes itself: "Your answering service takes a message. FirstRing takes
  the job."
- This is where the budget line already exists. Most of our first 38 customers come from here or
  from "owner carries the phone" (which costs marriages, not dollars — also a strong pitch).

### 2. AI receptionists, horizontal (SMB-generic)
**Goodcall, HeyRosie, Voctiv, and a long tail of Vapi/Retell wrappers**
- Price: $30–$300/mo, self-serve.
- Weakness: generic. They don't know what a "no-heat call" in January means, can't quote a dispatch
  fee, don't integrate with Housecall Pro/ServiceTitan dispatch boards, and put setup burden on the
  owner (who will not configure software, ever).
- Our wedge: trade-specific flows out of the box + white-glove setup ("live in 24 hours, we do
  everything"). Horizontal players can't afford to hand-hold a $200/mo account; as a founder-led
  company, for the first 100 customers, I can — and that becomes the install base that funds
  self-serve later.

### 3. AI receptionists, vertical home services (the direct fight)
**Avoca (HVAC-focused, ServiceTitan-aligned, well funded), Sameday AI (home services), plus
FSM-native AI: ServiceTitan contact-center AI, Jobber's AI features, Housecall Pro add-ons**
- These prove the thesis. Avoca in particular validates that HVAC owners pay real money for AI call
  handling.
- How we win anyway:
  - **They go upmarket; we go down.** Funded verticals chase $50M-revenue contractors and PE
    roll-ups (bigger ACVs, board-pleasing logos). The 5–50 employee shop gets a webinar and a
    self-serve form. We give that shop a founder's cell number.
  - **Price:** their packages commonly land $500–$2,000+/mo with onboarding fees. We start at $199.
  - **Platform-agnostic:** ServiceTitan-native tools lose every Jobber/Housecall Pro shop — which
    is exactly the smaller-shop segment we target. (Jobber + Housecall Pro together serve the SMB
    end of the market; that's our pond.)
- FSM platforms shipping native AI is the real 18–24 month threat. Response: list in their app
  marketplaces early (their marketplace teams *want* integrations; it's distribution for us),
  differentiate on call quality and multi-channel (calls + texts + web chat), and keep the option
  to expand to verticals the FSMs don't own.

### 4. DIY / infrastructure
**Vapi, Retell, Bland — "a tech-savvy nephew could build this"**
- True, and irrelevant for this buyer. A plumbing company owner will not maintain prompt flows,
  telephony config, and API integrations. They buy outcomes. (Agencies building one-off bots on
  these platforms are actually a future *channel* — see growth loops.)

## Positioning statement

> **For plumbing and HVAC companies with 5–50 employees** who lose jobs every week to missed calls,
> **FirstRing** is an AI receptionist **that answers every call and books the job straight into
> Housecall Pro, Jobber, or ServiceTitan** — unlike answering services that take messages and
> generic AI bots that don't know a no-heat emergency from a faucet drip. **Live in 24 hours. We do
> the setup. One saved job pays for it.**
